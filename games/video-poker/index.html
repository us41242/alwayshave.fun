How the coach scores a hold

For each of the 32 ways to hold a dealt hand, the trainer deals every possible replacement from the 47 unseen cards, pays each result on the 9/6 table, and averages. That is the exact expected value (EV) of the hold, in coins won per coin bet. The best hold is the one with the highest EV. Your cost on a hand is the gap between the best hold and yours; over a session the total cost, divided by hands, is how far you sit below the game's 99.54% return.

Two habits produce most of the cost. Holding a "kicker" with a pair (never right in Jacks or Better). And breaking a paying hand for a draw when the draw is worth less: a made flush is worth 6, four to a royal is worth about 18, so you break the flush; but a made straight against four to a straight flush stays, because the straight flush draw is worth about 3.

The "9/6" is the full house and flush payouts. Optimal play returns 99.54% on this table. The same game paying 8/5 returns 97.30%, and 6/5 returns 95.00%; before you sit down, read the full house and flush lines. The royal only pays 800-to-1 at five coins (it is 250 per coin at fewer), which is why the trainer assumes max coins. Play a lower denomination rather than fewer coins.
